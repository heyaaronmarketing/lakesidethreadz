What Is DTF Printing? A Plain-English Guide (2026)

DTF (Direct to Film) printing is the reason small embroidery shops can now print full-color logos on anything — no screens, no minimums, no Chinese factory. Here's what it actually is.

Get Your Quote →

The one-sentence definition

DTF stands for Direct-to-Film. It's a printing method where your full-color design is printed onto a special PET film, dusted with a hot-melt powder adhesive, and then heat-pressed onto the garment. The result is a vivid, full-color, soft-hand transfer that bonds permanently to cotton, polyester, blends, nylon, and even leather and canvas.

How DTF actually works (the four steps)

Step 1: A specialized printer lays down a full-color CMYK image onto clear PET film, then prints a dense white underbase on top of the color layer. Step 2: While the ink is still wet, a hot-melt adhesive powder gets sprinkled across the whole printed area and the excess is shaken off (it only sticks to the wet ink). Step 3: The powdered film gets cured at ~160°C for ~2 minutes, which melts the adhesive into a sticky backing. Step 4: The film is placed face-down on the garment and heat-pressed at ~160°C for ~15 seconds. The adhesive bonds the ink to the fabric; the film peels off, leaving the design permanently fused.

What DTF is good for

Full-color photo-quality prints that would be impossible with screen printing (no spot-color limit, no setup per color). Small runs where screen-printing setup fees would eat all the margin — DTF has essentially no setup cost per color, so 10 shirts is economic. Fabrics that screen printing struggles with — nylon jackets, polyester performance wear, blends, canvas bags. Full-front or full-back prints with a lot of visual detail (gradients, fine text, photographic elements).

What DTF is NOT good for

High-volume runs of simple 1-2 color designs — screen printing wins on per-piece cost above ~50 pieces for simple art. Ultra-soft water-based prints where the print has to feel like nothing — DTF has a slight rubbery hand you can feel if you run a finger over it (less than vinyl, more than screen). Heavy embroidered look + feel — if the client wants a raised stitch logo on a polo, that's embroidery, not DTF.

DTF vs screen printing vs embroidery — quick picker

DTF: full-color, low-quantity (1-50), any fabric, slight rubbery hand, prints in days. Screen printing: 1-6 colors, higher quantity (25+), cotton + 50/50 blends, soft water-based or plastisol hand, prints in 7-10 days. Embroidery: no color limit but texture-dependent, any quantity, cotton + twill + polyester + fleece, premium stitch hand, 5-10 day turnaround. For a 24-piece custom company tee run with a 4-color logo, DTF usually wins. For 100+ identical pieces, screen printing is cheaper per piece. For a logo on a left chest that should feel premium, embroidery.

What DTF typically costs

A single 11-inch full-color transfer costs around $4-8 for us to produce and press. Per-shirt pricing including the blank and labor usually lands at $10-18 for a basic cotton tee with a full-color logo. For a 25-piece run, expect around $12-15/piece. This is no-minimums territory — we'll do 1 or we'll do 500.

How to send us art for a DTF print

High-resolution PNG with transparent background works best (300 DPI at print size). Vector files (SVG, AI, PDF) are even better because we can scale without pixelation. Avoid low-res JPEGs pulled from a website — they'll look soft when printed. If all you have is a business card photo of your logo, we can clean it up or re-draw it for a small fee.

Frequently asked

Is DTF printing the same as DTG (Direct-to-Garment)?

No. DTG prints directly onto the fabric using a specialized inkjet — no film, no transfer. DTG is great for 100% cotton at low quantities but has limits (can't print white ink on dark garments as reliably, slower than DTF, higher equipment cost). DTF is a transfer process and works on more fabrics.

How long does a DTF print last on a shirt?

A properly pressed DTF print lasts 40-60+ wash cycles with normal care. Wash cold, inside out, air dry if you want to maximize print lifespan. Most customers' shirts outlive the print; some report prints still looking sharp after 100+ washes.

Can DTF be done on dark shirts?

Yes — that's actually one of its strongest use cases. The dense white underbase lets full-color designs pop on black, navy, or any dark fabric. Screen printing on dark shirts requires a separate white underbase print, which doubles the setup cost. DTF handles it in one pass.

What's the minimum order for DTF printing?

No minimum. We'll run one shirt. One of the main reasons DTF exists is to make low-quantity custom apparel economic — we don't charge screen-setup fees because there are no screens.

Does DTF work on hats, bags, or jackets?

Yes on canvas bags, nylon or cotton jackets, polyester performance wear, and heavier canvas pieces. For hats, DTF works on flat-panel foam fronts but is less common — embroidery or heat-transfer vinyl is more typical for hats.

How is DTF different from HTV (iron-on vinyl)?

HTV is a single-color plastic vinyl cut into shapes and pressed on. It's cheap and durable but one color per layer and limited to flat shapes. DTF prints photographic full-color detail including fine text and gradients that vinyl can't reproduce.
