The Spirit Wear Fundraiser Guide for Booster Clubs

How to run a profitable spirit wear fundraiser without losing a weekend to Venmo chaos. Playbook, price math, and lessons from 20+ Texas booster runs.

Get Your Quote →

What a spirit wear fundraiser actually is

A spirit wear fundraiser is a time-limited sale of school-branded apparel where the booster club collects orders from families, pays the vendor wholesale, and keeps the markup. Done well, a single fall-sports run clears $2,000-8,000 for the program. Done poorly, the president burns 15 hours chasing sizes, parents complain about late deliveries, and the booster swears off doing it again.

The 5-step playbook that works

Step 1: Pick 3-5 pieces, not 20. Trucker hats, a hoodie, a long-sleeve dri-fit, maybe a youth size tee. Too many options confuses parents and multiplies your packing complexity. Step 2: Set up one simple online form (Google Form is fine) with piece, size, color, qty, and a text field for the student's name if items go to individuals. Step 3: Open a 2-3 week ordering window — any shorter and parents don't notice; any longer and energy dies. Step 4: Send 3 reminders: launch, midpoint, 48 hours before close. Step 5: Close the window, send one big order to the vendor, distribute at a parent-pickup night 2-3 weeks later.

The margin math

Say your vendor wholesale cost for an embroidered trucker hat is $12 and you sell it to families at $22. That's $10 profit per hat. 100 hats = $1,000 profit for the program. Add hoodies at $22 wholesale selling at $40 = $18 profit, 50 hoodies = $900 more. Add long-sleeve dri-fit at $15 wholesale selling at $25 = $10 profit, 75 shirts = $750. Total 225-piece run clears about $2,650. For comparison: a typical restaurant-night fundraiser clears $300-800. Spirit wear scales better per hour of volunteer time.

Pitfalls that kill the margin

Over-ordering inventory you'll be stuck with (solution: only order what pre-sold). Taking cash without a tracking system (solution: Venmo + a spreadsheet, or a form that integrates with payment). Letting the ordering window stay open 'while we wait on a few more orders' (solution: hard close date, always). Trying to source everything from different suppliers (solution: one vendor who can do hats + shirts + hoodies so you pay one invoice). Printing without proper district approval for the mark (solution: get the comms office email on file first; see our school pages for context).

We set up the Google Form for you (saves 2 hours), we quote in wholesale so you know exactly what to charge, we pack orders per-student so your pickup night is bags-not-piles, and we handle the district mark approval conversation with the comms office if you've never done it before. When to run the fundraiser

Fall sports (August launch, September pickup): biggest window because football, volleyball, cheer all overlap. Winter sports (November launch, December pickup): hoodies + beanies sell well, feeds into holiday gifting. Spring sports (February launch): baseball + softball + track + golf. Avoid May-July (families tapped out from end-of-year costs). Spirit wear + Project Grad shirts can run concurrently in the spring with cross-promotion.

Frequently asked

How much does a typical booster spirit wear fundraiser raise?

Varies by school size. Small schools (under 500 students, athletic boosters only): $800-2,500 per run. Mid-sized (500-1,500 students): $2,500-6,000. Large (1,500+): $6,000-15,000. Band and Project Grad fundraisers scale similarly.

Do we need to buy inventory in advance?

No — the modern approach is pre-order only. Parents order through a Google Form or similar; you place one bulk order with the vendor after the window closes; the booster has zero inventory risk.

What if parents want sizes we didn't order?

Build the form to let families select from all adult + youth sizes. The vendor orders exactly what pre-sold. Don't try to predict size distribution — let parents do it.

How long from ordering window close to pickup night?

Typically 2-3 weeks for production + packing. Add a week for shipping to the booster president. Schedule the pickup night at least 4 weeks after the ordering window closes.

Can families order individually and have it shipped to them?

Possible but more complex. Most booster clubs batch-ship to the president and do a single pickup night; parents who miss pickup night get theirs at a backup pickup the following week. Individual shipping adds per-piece shipping cost and reduces the booster's margin.

How do we handle the school logo approval?

Most Texas districts require written vendor approval for the licensed mark. An email from your district's communications or athletics office is usually enough. If you're not sure who to ask, we'll help — we've done this with 10+ Texas schools.
